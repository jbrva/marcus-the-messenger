The Autumn Mystery

A puzzle hiding on every calendar you'll ever use

Marcus is helping the Emperor's chief scholar sort the calendar of festivals. He notices something strange about the last four months of the year.

Step I
The word September comes from the Latin word septem. What does septem mean?
Step II
Right. Now look at your calendar. September is which month of the year?
Step III — The Puzzle

The Answer

Long ago, the Roman year started in March, not January. The calendar had only ten months — spring through autumn — and the dark winter days had no months at all.

Then King Numa added two new months at the end of the year — January and February — to cover the winter. Later, they were moved to the beginning. Everything else shifted two positions down:

So October means eight, but is month ten, because January and February bumped it forward two places. The mystery is hiding on every calendar you'll ever use. Now you know.
